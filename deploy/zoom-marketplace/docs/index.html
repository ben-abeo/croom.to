Crystal Meet Rooms for Zoom

Add, use and remove the Zoom integration

Crystal Meet Rooms lets a Crystal Meet conference room join Zoom meetings. When someone presses Join on the room's touch screen, the room's device joins as a participant through Zoom's Meeting SDK.

Each Crystal Meet room has a small computer behind its TV (the room's device), a touch screen on the table that shows the room page, and a sign by the door. A person in the room presses Join on the room page, and within about fifteen seconds the Zoom meeting appears on the TV, with the room's camera and speakerphone. The room joins as its own Zoom user, named after the room. Nobody signs in on the device and nothing is installed inside Zoom: an administrator sets the integration up once for the Zoom account and once for each room.

Adding the appConnect your Zoom account and each room: two apps, a room user, a file on the device. Using the appJoin, mute, turn the camera off and leave from the room page. Removing the appTake the credentials off the device, remove the apps, and what changes.

What the integration accesses

It reads one thing from Zoom: a Zoom Access Key (ZAK) for the room's own Zoom user, which lets the room join meetings as that user. It does not record meetings or keep their audio, video, chat or participant lists, and every connection to Zoom uses HTTPS with TLS 1.2 or higher. What stays on the room's device is set out in the privacy policy.

For the Zoom account's administrator

Adding the app

Setting up the Zoom side takes about 30 minutes in the Zoom App Marketplace and admin portal, once for your Zoom account; each room then takes about five minutes. There is no sign-in or consent screen: you authorize the integration by creating and activating its two apps and putting their credentials on the room's device.

Before you begin

  • Access. Sign in as the Zoom account owner, or as an admin whose role has the Zoom for developers permission. The owner grants it at admin.zoom.us under User Management › Roles: edit the admin's role, then Role Settings › Advanced features › Zoom for developers, tick View and Edit. With it, Develop shows at the top of the App Marketplace.
  • Account and device. A paid Zoom account to which you can add users, and the room's Crystal Meet device installed on your network, as described in the room setup guide.
  • Secrets. You will collect two client IDs, two client secrets and an account ID. Keep them out of email and chat; they go straight into a file on each device.

  1. Create the Marketplace app with the Meeting SDK

    1. Sign in to the App Marketplace at marketplace.zoom.us, open Develop at the top right and choose Build App.
    2. Choose General App and press Create.
    3. On Basic Information, name the app Crystal Meet Rooms and choose Admin-managed where the page asks how the app is managed.
    4. Under App Credentials you will find a Development and a Production pair of Client ID and Client Secret. The rooms use the Production pair, which Zoom approves in its app review; until the approval, the Development pair joins meetings hosted on your own Zoom account only.
    5. If the page asks for an OAuth Redirect URL, enter https://www.crystalpm.com/. The rooms never use it.
    6. Open Features and its Embed tab, switch Meeting SDK on and save. Leave the three switches beneath it off: programmatic join use case, Request Anonymous Join Exception and Use Device OAuth.
    7. On Scopes, press Add Scopes, search for zak and add user:read:zak:admin (View a user's ZAK). In Scope Description write: "Fetches a conference-room user's ZAK so the room device can join meetings hosted by other Zoom accounts as that user." Add no other scope.
    8. Leave Access, Surface, Connect and Actions and Triggers as they are: the integration shows nothing inside the Zoom client and subscribes to no events.

    You should now see the app under Manage › Created Apps with the Meeting SDK on.

  2. Create the Server-to-Server OAuth app for tokens

    1. In the App Marketplace choose Develop › Build App, then Server-to-Server OAuth, press Create and name the app Crystal Meet room tokens.
    2. From App Credentials, keep the Account ID, Client ID and Client Secret for step 4.
    3. On Information, fill in a short description, the company name and a developer contact name and email; Zoom does not activate the app without them. On Feature, leave event subscriptions off.
    4. On Scopes, press Add Scopes and add two read-only scopes: search for zak and add user:read:zak:admin (View a user's ZAK), then search for token and add user:read:token:admin (View a user's token). The list offers only the scopes your own role allows; if either is missing, do this step as the account owner.
    5. On Activation, activate the app. Its tokens work only while it is active.

    You should now see the app listed as active.

  3. Create a Zoom user for the room

    1. At admin.zoom.us go to User Management › Users › Add Users. Use a mailbox you can read, for example room1@crystalpm.com, and the Basic user type, which is free on a paid account.
    2. Open the invitation email in that mailbox and finish the sign-up.
    3. In the user's profile, set the display name to the room's name, for example Room 1. That is the name hosts and participants see when the room joins.
    4. Repeat for each room.

    You should now see one active Basic user per room, each named after its room. A room user never hosts meetings, so it needs no paid licence.

  4. Put the credentials on the room's device

    1. Copy deploy/rooms/zoom-credentials.example.json from the Crystal Meet software to a file named zoom-credentials.json and fill in its six values. Each room gets its own file, because room_user differs.
      ValueWhere it comes from
      sdk_client_id, sdk_client_secretStep 1: the Production Client ID and Client Secret (the Development pair until Zoom has approved the app)
      account_id, s2s_client_id, s2s_client_secretStep 2: the Account ID, Client ID and Client Secret
      room_userStep 3: that room's Zoom user, for example room1@crystalpm.com
    2. Copy the file to the device, for example scp zoom-credentials.json pi@crystal-meet-room-1.local:~/.
    3. On a device you are installing now, add --zoom-credentials ~/zoom-credentials.json to the installer command from the room setup guide. On a device that is already installed, type sudo install -o pi -g pi -m 600 ~/zoom-credentials.json /etc/croom/zoom-credentials.json (your username instead of pi), then sudo systemctl restart croom.
    4. Delete the copy in your home folder: rm ~/zoom-credentials.json.

  5. Check the connection

    On the device, type /opt/croom/venv/bin/croom --check-zoom -c /etc/croom/config.yaml. It tries each credential with Zoom and reads:

    Zoom Meeting SDK: app <your app's client ID>, signature minted (Zoom checks it only when a meeting is joined)
Zoom account: server-to-server token obtained for account <your account ID>
Zoom room user: room1@crystalpm.com, ZAK obtained (valid 2 hours)
Ready: this room can join meetings on your own Zoom account now; meetings hosted by other accounts also need Zoom's approval of the SDK app, and the first outside-hosted meeting proves it.

    You should now see the check end with "Ready". If it does not, its last line says what to fix; see Troubleshooting.

For everyone in the room

Using the app

Prerequisites

  • A Zoom account that the room's Zoom user belongs to, with the two apps from Adding the app active.
  • The room's device switched on and on the network, with internet access to Zoom, and its check ending with "Ready".
  • A meeting to join: a booking on the room's calendar with a Zoom link in it, or a Zoom invitation link (or a meeting ID and its passcode) to enter on the room page.
  • For meetings hosted by other Zoom accounts, such as customers and vendors: the app's Production Client ID and Client Secret on the device, once Zoom has approved the app.

Join a meeting

  • From a booking. The room page, on the touch screen on the table, lists today's bookings under Today. Ten minutes before a booking with a Zoom link starts, the page shows it with Join now, and its row in the list shows Join; earlier, the button reads Join opens at and the time. Press Join now. If the booking's Zoom link carries no passcode, the room uses the one written in the booking's invitation text (for example "Passcode: 123456"); if there is none and the meeting wants one, the join fails and the room page asks for it (see "If Zoom refuses" below).
  • From a link or a meeting ID. Paste the meeting's full invitation link, passcode included, into Join with a link and press Join. Or type the meeting ID in the box: a Passcode field appears under it, and the meeting's passcode goes there before you press Join. The field appears for a meeting ID and for a Zoom link that has no passcode in it, not for a link that carries its passcode or for a Google Meet link; leave it empty for a meeting that has no passcode. A passcode may be letters, digits and @ - _ *, up to 32 characters. The keyboard button beside the box opens an on-screen keyboard, and on the table screen tapping the Passcode field opens it on its digits.
  • What happens. The room page shows Joining while the room's screen connects. Within about fifteen seconds Zoom's meeting appears on the TV and the room page shows In a meeting with the time since the room joined. The room appears in the meeting's participant list under its own name, for example Room 1. If the host uses a waiting room, the room page says "Waiting for the host to let the room in." until the host admits the room. Cancel stops a join.
  • If Zoom refuses. The room page shows Couldn't join with Zoom's reason. If the reason begins "Zoom needs the meeting passcode", the card keeps the link and shows the Passcode field, with a Join again button beside Dismiss: type the passcode and press Join again. For any other reason, press Dismiss and see Troubleshooting.

Nothing joins by itself: the room joins a meeting only when someone presses Join.

During the meeting

On the room pageWhat it does
Mute / UnmuteMutes or unmutes the room's microphone in the meeting; the host and participants see the room muted.
Turn camera off / Turn camera onStops or starts the room's video in the meeting.
Leave, then Tap again to leaveThe room leaves the meeting; the second tap, within five seconds, confirms. The TV returns to its idle screen.

The Sound and Camera sections of the same page set the speaker's volume and the camera's framing. When the host ends the meeting, the room returns to idle by itself.

What the TV shows

During a meeting, Zoom's meeting view, full screen. Between meetings, the screen chosen under TV when idle on the room page: Information (the room's name, whether it is free, the next booking, the time, and "Press Join on the controller to start a meeting"), Quiet (the room's name and status), Brand (the Crystal PM logo) or Bounce (the logo drifting across the screen).

What the door sign shows

The sign by the door shows the room's name and its status in colour: green Available with the time until the next booking, amber Starting soon in the ten minutes before a booking, and red Booked while a booking is in progress or In use while the room is in a meeting. Below that it lists the next bookings of the day.

Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat to do
There is no Develop menu in the App MarketplaceYour role lacks the Zoom for developers permission. The account owner adds it under User Management › Roles (Role Settings › Advanced features), or creates the two apps personally.
The check says "not configured"It names the value that is missing or still a placeholder in /etc/croom/zoom-credentials.json. The three server-to-server values and room_user go together: fill in all four or none.
The check says Zoom refused the credentialsThe account ID, client ID or client secret of the Server-to-Server app is wrong, or the app is not activated. Copy them again from App Credentials and check the app's Activation page.
The check says there is no such user, or that a scope is missingroom_user must be an active user on this Zoom account. A missing scope is quoted in Zoom's own words: add that scope to the Server-to-Server app under Scopes and activate the app again.
The room page says the meeting is not authorizedThe meeting is hosted by another Zoom account. Three things must all be true: Zoom has approved the app, the device holds its Production Client ID and Client Secret, and the credentials file has the server-to-server values and room_user. Fix what is missing, restart the service with sudo systemctl restart croom and run the check again. If all three are true and Zoom still refuses, contact Crystal PM support.
Wrong passcode, or meeting not foundPaste the full invitation link with its passcode, or type the passcode in the Passcode field that appears under the link box for a meeting ID or a link without one. After "Couldn't join" with "Zoom needs the meeting passcode", type the right passcode in the field the card shows and press Join again. Zoom's exact reason shows on the room page.
The room page says it is waiting for the hostThe meeting has a waiting room. The host admits the room from the participant list, and the room page switches to In a meeting.
The TV stays dark and the room page says Zoom's SDK did not loadThe device could not reach source.zoom.us. Check the device's internet access and any web filter, then try again.

For the Zoom account's administrator

Removing the app

Removing the integration takes a few minutes per room and a few minutes in Zoom. Step 1 on its own already stops a room from joining Zoom meetings; steps 2 and 3 take the integration out of your Zoom account.

  1. Take the credentials off each room's device

    1. Delete the credentials file: sudo rm /etc/croom/zoom-credentials.json.
    2. Optional, to take Zoom off the room page as well: open the device's settings with sudo nano /etc/croom/config.yaml and, under meeting:, remove zoom from platforms, for example platforms: [google_meet].
    3. Restart the service: sudo systemctl restart croom.
    4. Check: /opt/croom/venv/bin/croom --check-zoom -c /etc/croom/config.yaml now says "Zoom Meeting SDK not configured: credentials file not found or unreadable: /etc/croom/zoom-credentials.json".

  2. Deactivate or delete the two Marketplace apps

    1. Sign in to marketplace.zoom.us as the account owner or the admin who created the apps, and open Manage › Created Apps.
    2. Crystal Meet room tokens (Server-to-Server OAuth): open it and deactivate it on its Activation page, or delete it from the ⋯ menu at the end of its row. Its credentials stop working at once, so no device can get a token or a ZAK with them.
    3. Crystal Meet Rooms (General App with the Meeting SDK): delete it from the ⋯ menu at the end of its row. If it was also added to your account during setup (Local Test › Add App Now), remove it under Manage › Added Apps: click the app, then Remove.

  3. Remove the room's Zoom user, if you no longer need it

    At admin.zoom.us go to User Management › Users, find the room's user, for example room1@crystalpm.com, and deactivate or delete it from the menu at the end of its row. The integration never used it to host, schedule or record anything.

What changes

  • Rooms can no longer join Zoom meetings. With the optional change in step 1, the room page offers no Join for Zoom bookings and refuses Zoom links as "Unsupported meeting link". Without it, Zoom bookings still show Join, but Zoom turns the room away: its web client does not admit automated guests.
  • Everything else keeps working: Google Meet, today's bookings on the room page, the Sound and Camera controls, the TV's idle screens and the door sign.
  • Nothing of yours stays on Zoom's side. The integration stores nothing at Zoom. Deactivating or deleting the apps revokes their credentials, and the tokens the device fetched expire on their own: the account token within an hour, the room's ZAK within two hours. The room's Zoom user stays in your account until you remove it in step 3.
  • On the device, deleting the credentials file ends the integration's access to Zoom. What remains is the device's service log, which notes the meetings the room joined and ages out with the system's log rotation. The TV's browser never kept the join details: the device serves them with no-store.

To use Zoom in the rooms again, follow Adding the app once more.
